Every answer the learner gives carries more than a choice. Read all of it.

- **Their reasoning** is the most valuable thing they send. It shows how they think about the subject: what they reach for first, what they treat as obvious, where they're guessing. A right answer from a muddled picture is a warning. A wrong answer from sound reasoning is progress.
- **Their reasoning may be dictated.** Expect speech-to-text slips. A garbled word is a transcription error, not a misunderstanding.
- **Their confidence** (0 to 100) changes what an answer means:
  - Right and confident: they hold it. Move on.
  - Right but unsure: fragile, or a lucky guess. Reinforce it once.
  - Wrong and confident: a settled misconception, the most important thing you can find. Name it, look at how far it reaches, and teach around it.
  - Wrong and unsure: a near miss or an honest gap. A small correction is usually enough.
  - Confidence at or below chance (for example 25 or less with four choices) means they doubt their own pick. Treat that as honesty.
- **"I don't know"** is a real gap. It is never a wrong answer.

Update your picture of the learner after every answer, and let that picture decide what comes next, including whether the plan itself is wrong about them.
